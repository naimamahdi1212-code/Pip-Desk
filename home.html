Four words, defined

The vocabulary you actually need

Forex talk sounds harder than it is. These four terms cover most of what you'll run into in your first month — each with a real example so it sticks.

TERM 01

Pip

The smallest standard price move for a currency pair — how traders measure a change without arguing over decimal places.

EXAMPLE

EUR/USD moves from 1.0842 to 1.0843 — that's a one‑pip move.

TERM 02

Leverage

Borrowed buying power that lets a small deposit control a much larger position. It scales your gains — and your losses — the same way.

EXAMPLE

With 10:1 leverage, $500 controls a $5,000 position. A 2% move is a 20% swing on your money.

TERM 03

Spread

The gap between the price you can sell at (bid) and the price you can buy at (ask). It's the built-in cost of placing a trade.

EXAMPLE

GBP/USD bid 1.2650, ask 1.2653 — a 3‑pip spread, paid just by entering.

TERM 04

Candlestick

A single bar showing a pair's opening price, closing price, and the highest and lowest points it touched in one time window.

EXAMPLE

One mint‑green hourly candle means the price finished that hour higher than it started.

Chart reading, slowed down

How to read a candlestick

Every candle tells you four numbers at a glance. Colour tells you which direction the price travelled — the shape tells you how far.

Body
The thick block. Its edges mark the open and close prices for that period.
Wick
The thin lines above and below the body. They mark the highest and lowest prices reached.
Open
The price at the very start of that time period.
Close
The price at the very end of that time period.

Your first month

A learning path, in order

This sequence matters — each step gives you something the next one depends on. Don't skip to step three.

  1. 01

    Learn the vocabulary first

    Get comfortable with pip, spread, leverage, and lot size before you open a live chart. Half of trading confusion is just unfamiliar words.

  2. 02

    Practice reading candlestick charts

    Pull up a free chart and spot the open, close, high, and low on real candles. Do this until it's boring — that's when it's useful.

  3. 03

    Place trades on a demo account

    Most brokers offer a free demo account funded with fake money. Use it to learn order types and platform mechanics with nothing at risk.

  4. 04

    Write down a simple risk rule

    Decide, in writing, the maximum you're willing to risk on any one trade — before you ever fund a real account. This one habit outlasts every strategy.
